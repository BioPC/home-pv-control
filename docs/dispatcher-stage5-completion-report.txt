2. DISPATCHER ARCHITECTURE
execution/dispatcher.py is the sole owner of executable simulation sequencing.
Control, HBC, inverter, entity and coordinator modules do not issue actuator actions.
The dispatcher requires exactly FakeExecutor; no service-backed executor is supplied.
Separate inverter and HBC lane locks allow an HBC operation alongside one inverter
operation. Inverter physical service operations are serial. Immutable registered
proposals own sequence identity and controller/configuration provenance.
Identical pending work coalesces without resetting identity or accepted time. New
intent supersedes only entirely unsent eligible batches. An issued sequence retains
its remaining steps, including during safe-disable draining. Failure isolates the
remaining unsent steps for that controller. Already queued unrelated controllers can
continue after known terminal transport, while durable uncertainty remains restrictive.
Live read-only setup does not automatically instantiate the fake dispatcher or invent
simulated physical events from real telemetry. Real-HA tests explicitly inject it.

3. FAKE EXECUTOR INTERFACE
execution/executor.py defines immutable ExecutionRequest, IssueReceipt, Completion
and QuiescenceReceipt data and an Executor protocol. FakeExecutor is the only concrete
implementation. It accepts immutable command steps, records requests, permits delayed
receipt and controlled completion, duplicates, stale/old-owner callbacks, transport
failure, missing completion and explicit quiescence. Test checkpoints inject crashes
at durable boundaries. It has no HA service, HTTP-write or MQTT-write implementation.

4. DURABLE OPERATION LIFECYCLE
The execution ledger remains authoritative. PREPARED commits before queue admission;
DISPATCH_ARMED, owner/configuration/dispatch identity and time commit before invocation.
A matching fake issue receipt permits SERVICE_IN_FLIGHT and accepted-event recording.
Completion commits SERVICE_COMPLETED, then AWAITING_VERIFICATION or OPEN_LOOP.
Independent proof permits VERIFIED; confirmed settling permits RETIRED for PV main
work. Native select/switch steps retire after independent value confirmation.
SUPERSEDED_UNSENT, SERVICE_FAILED, VERIFICATION_FAILED, UNVERIFIABLE_OPEN_LOOP,
OUTCOME_UNKNOWN and RECOVERY_REQUIRED remain explicit. Commit failure means no call
when arming failed. A call/commit ambiguity becomes UNKNOWN, never an automatic replay.
Age does not clear work. Duplicate/stale callbacks must match epoch, configuration,
controller, dispatch and exact step payload before they can advance an operation.
An explicit quiescence receipt resolves transport continuity only. It cannot resolve
the physical restoration obligation. Proven completed transport may resume independent
verification through an explicit recovery transition.

The checksummed fake_dispatch companion stores immutable registered plans/configuration
references, bounded simulation audit history and settling state. Missing journal beside
existing operations fails closed. Known dev.4 execution records migrate additively after
checksum validation; arbitrary missing/extra fields and corruption remain rejected.

5. HBC EXECUTION LANE
The fake HBC lane orders strategy then goal on entry, goal then strategy on exit.
Durable captured originals precede forced action registration and issue. The ARMED
ledger commit also owns originals and possible-issuance uncertainty. A crash after
the fake call but before accepted-event persistence cannot discard that ownership.
Possible issuance is never misreported as an accepted/issued fact.
Fresh independent native observations must match the issued entity, option, owner,
configuration, dispatch and timestamps. Executor success alone never confirms a select.
Known HBC verification uses its own snapshot lane and does not freeze inverter policy.
Unknown HBC physical outcomes stay restrictive. A fresh explicit policy retry is allowed
after completed transport failure; age cannot replay an uncertain HBC operation.
Independent observation of both saved originals after their applicable possible writes
clears captured ownership. Fault/helper intents remain data; no real services are added.

6. COOLDOWN AND ACCEPTED-COMMAND INTEGRATION
Only matched execution-owned simulated acceptance updates dev.4 accepted-command facts.
Validation uses the immutable registered proposal rather than rebinding old work to a
newer policy evaluation. It checks targets, observed before-values, controller/config
identity, dispatch/owner identity, clock continuity, capture token and cooldown duration.
A CommandBatch, PREPARED record, pre-action or memory commit cannot start main PV cooldown.
The accepted main issue starts it. HBC events use their separate lane and cannot invent
PV cooldown. Crash uncertainty remains restrictive even if acceptance could not commit.
Maintenance full restoration has a narrow owned-registration path after an accepted
disabled observation; it accepts only the archived full target and matching provenance.

7. VERIFICATION, FINALIZATION AND SETTLING
Production pure verification interprets independent fake evidence. Covered outcomes:
service success + matching readback; stale evidence; wrong target; unavailable readback;
transport failure; outcome unknown; open-loop controllers; OpenDTU post-dispatch API
poll/status/device/serial evidence; and Enphase enable/Production readiness plus limit
readback. Wrong-target final failure remains a durable failure with a restore obligation.
Generic Action pre/main/post sequencing and partial failures are covered. Opaque actions
without independent readback remain open-loop; transport success cannot be physical proof.
Settling consumes fresh PV after verified limits, uses accepted monotonic elapsed time
within a boot, retains completed settling across reload, and resets partial sampling
continuity. A newer issued command explicitly supersedes the older interval without
claiming that the old physical response occurred. Timeout is not restoration proof.

8. RESTORATION OBLIGATIONS AND ALREADY-FULL PROOF
Obligations retain archived controller UID, configuration signature/reference, full
target, establishment time, owner and physical-operation references. They survive removal,
reconfiguration, zero-controller rediscovery, restart, reload, failure and owner change.
Failure and unknown status remain explicit; they are never erased because the fleet changed.
Resolution requires matched independent full-output evidence after conflicting possible
work is quiescent. Old proof cannot resolve a newer conflicting operation. Replacement
identity/signature, command cache, startup assumption and stale full state are rejected.
The already-full shortcut requires a matching fresh independently verifiable live adapter
view and exact archived identity/range. Unstructured Enphase/OpenDTU observations are
refused in favor of the complete structured evidence path. The approved durable Night
Restore proof exception is identity-bound and cannot cover later conflicting activity.

9. MAINTENANCE TRANSACTIONS
Safe-disable captures original enable intent, transaction identity/token, owner epoch,
request clock and every applicable current/historical restoration identity. A fresh
accepted post-disable evaluation is mandatory. Unknown plant ownership is restrictive.
Maintenance drains/reconciles work, resolves every applicable full-output obligation,
requires confirmed HBC restoration and verified settling or the bounded Night proof
exception. Exactly 180 monotonic seconds times out to BLOCKED, never authorization.
A positive maintenance decision is a simulation result tied to owner, ledger revision
and deadline; physical-write authorization remains false. It performs no update,
uninstall or removal. Restart blocks the transaction and preserves obligations.
Explicit recovery requires the old token and a fresh accepted disabled observation,
creates a new token/deadline and retains obligations and original saved enable intent.
It cannot silently resume an old authorization or clear unknown transport.
